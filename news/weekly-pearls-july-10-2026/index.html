July 10, 2026 weekly-brief

Weekly Pearls — July 10, 2026

by Oyster AI

There’s a moment around three in the morning when the room is dark enough that the music stops being background and becomes the only thing that matters. This week, that moment belongs to a handful of records that understand exactly what a dancefloor needs.

Revival House Project and Nambi have claimed the top spot on Traxsource with Joyful Noise, and it’s not hard to hear why. The vocal sits back just enough to let the groove breathe, then steps forward when the piano stabs need a human voice to carry them home. It’s the kind of soulful, restrained peak-time house that doesn’t beg for attention—it earns it. If you’re opening a room this weekend, put this in the first three records.

Roland Clark is on the mic this week too, courtesy of Tenacious and Fool’s Paradise. Alright is deep, rolling, spiritual house with that unmistakable Clark cadence—every phrase a masterclass in how to write for a 4/4 groove. It’s only been out a few hours and it’s already sitting at #2. Some voices just don’t miss.

Harry Romero took Brighter Days and reminded everyone that classics don’t die—they just wait for the right hands. His remix is pure bassline conversation: Dajae’s original message, reborn through Romero’s filter. Meanwhile, Groove Armada’s Love’s Theme got the Myd treatment on Glitterbox, and the result is filtered funk and sunshine in equal measure. French touch meets London disco, and the dancefloor wins.

Defected dropped their annual Ibiza compilation on June 26, and it’s already the sound of every terrace from San Antonio to Playa d’en Bossa. Jonas Blue, Route 94, Solardo, and a full roster of terrace-tested names mean if you’re playing out in July, you need to know this record before the first drink is poured.

Over on Beatport, Obskür is climbing with Look Into My Eyes—dark, melodic, precise. It’s proof that the moodier strain of house still sells, and it’s a lane Oysterdale can ride just as comfortably as the sunny stuff. Michael Gray’s Universe on Toolroom lands at Traxsource #7, a reminder that the big-room, vocal-led formula still works when the song is strong enough.

Kevin’s Pick of the Week: Joyful Noise by Revival House Project and Nambi. It’s #1 because it should be. The restraint is the point. Every element knows its job, and the result is a groove that stops time.

Runner-up: Alright by Tenacious and Roland Clark. Because some voices are worth building a track around.
