The light is already shifting in Oslo, that late-August blue that makes every club night feel like it might be the last one of summer. Inside, though, the house is still warm. This week the floor belongs to voices — old souls reimagined, gospel reaching across a crowd, Swedish patience winning out over noise.

Defected has done what Defected does best: it took a moment and turned it into a record. The viral Coachella performance of “Natural Blues” with Jacob Lusk, BLOND:ISH and Kiko Franco now has an official studio cut, **“Natural Blues (TECIE)”**, and it lands with the same church-raised, hands-in-the-air energy that stopped a festival field. A few days earlier, Detroit’s own **Inner City** returned to Glitterbox with **“Turn Me On”** featuring Steffanie Christi’an — a velvet, unhurried reminder that classic songwriting still owns the room. And because the groove needs teeth too, **Route 94**’s “4Get The Girl” has been reworked by **Mellizos** into a tougher, rolling 2026 version that already feels like a peak-time staple.

Over in Scandinavia, the signal is quieter but just as strong. **MOLØ**’s debut album **Fold** arrives on Studio Barnhus on August 24, eight tracks of deep, patient house that move like a night slowly finding its shape. Norwegian vocalist **Ane Brun** appears on “Malmö,” and the whole record feels like an argument for letting music breathe — something I keep coming back to when I’m producing. Studio Barnhus also has **Kornél Kovács**’ debut LP lined up for August 26, so Swedish deep house is having a real late-summer moment.

**Kevin’s Pick of the Week:** Inner City feat. Steffanie Christi’an — “Turn Me On.” It’s the one I keep returning to. No tricks, just a great voice, a great bassline, and the kind of mood that makes a small room feel enormous.
