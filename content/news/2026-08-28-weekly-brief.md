We made it to the last Friday of August. The sun is still hanging on, but the clubs are already sharpening their knives for autumn — and this week the floor is ruled by voices, warm basslines, and the kind of disco-house records that feel like they were made for the moment the lights go down.

Defected lands the first punch. **RUZE & Chesster**’s **“Another Night”** gets the Ranger Trucco remix treatment today, and it is exactly what a peak-time Defected record should be: a vocal that climbs, piano stabs that bite, and a drop built for raised hands. A little further down the soulful road, **Milk & Sugar** and **James Hurr** join forces on **“Got So High,”** a Fool's Paradise cut that sounds like late-afternoon sun hitting the dancefloor. It is sitting at #4 on the Traxsource House chart this week, and it is easy to hear why — the groove is patient, the vocal is effortless, and the whole thing refuses to rush.

There is strength in the classics, too. **Kathy Brown**, **Jet Boot Jack**, and **Mike Newman** bring **“Bringing The Good Times Back,”** and the title is not lying. Brown’s voice does what it has always done: it cuts through the mix and makes the room feel smaller and bigger at the same time. Meanwhile, **Inner City** and **LOSTBOYJAY** keep rebuilding the **“Good Life”** blueprint for Armada, and it still lands like a promise every time the chorus hits.

Scandinavia is quieter this week, but the signal is still there if you listen. **MOLØ**’s debut album *Fold* is now out on Studio Barnhus, and the patient, deep-house mood of it feels like the right soundtrack for an Oslo September. Norwegian vocalist **Ane Brun** appears on “Malmö,” and the whole record is a reminder that restraint can hit harder than noise. There are no major Nordic chart placements this week, but that kind of breathing room is its own kind of momentum — the calm before the clubs fill up again.

**Kevin’s Pick of the Week:** Milk & Sugar, James Hurr — **“Got So High.”** It lands right in the sweet spot: a feel-good vocal, a rolling bassline, and enough disco shimmer to work at 9pm or 3am. The kind of record that does not ask for attention — it earns it.
