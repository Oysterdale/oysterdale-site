There is a particular warmth when a familiar voice lands on a new record and immediately sounds at home. This week the house floor is leaning into that warmth — vocal cuts, remix packages, and the kind of grooves that feel lived-in from the first bar.

**Inner City** stepping back onto **Glitterbox** with **Steffanie Christi'an** on *“Turn Me On”* is the headline. It is not a nostalgia exercise; it is a record built for the same rooms that once played *“Good Life”*, only now the low end is deeper and the arrangement knows when to breathe. A few days earlier, **Route 94** landed *“4Get The Girl”* on **Defected**, and the **Mellizos** remix quickly became the version to watch — tougher drums, a rolling undercurrent, and a hook that still sits center stage. It is exactly the kind of reinterpretation that keeps a single alive across different floors.

On the soulful side, **Kiko Navarro** stretched **Louie Vega & Elements Of Life**’s *“Sunshine (I Can Fly)”* into an eight-minute sunrise trip for **Vega Records**, currently riding high on Traxsource. **John Morales** and **Brian Lucas** also delivered *“All My Love (M+M Main Mix)”*, a reminder that Morales’ orchestral touch still belongs in the Top 10. For those who prefer the deeper lane, **Kevin Yost**’s *“Rising”* on **i! Records** is patient, dubby, and built for the long blend — the understated pearl that often ages best.

Labels are busy. **Defected** also revealed full details for **MelÉ’s** debut album *Alive On Arrival*, and the wider family is clearly betting on song-led house this month. **Toolroom**’s latest roll-out — **Bruno Furlan**, **Wh0**, **James Hurr & Jay Rah**, **David Tort** — is more peak-time and club-focused, but it confirms the market is hungry for functional, well-produced ammunition. **Beatport**’s August Best New House and Best New Hype House charts reflect the same split: soulful pearls on one side, tight rolling tools on the other.

**Kevin’s Pick of the Week:** *Louie Vega & Elements Of Life feat. Raul Midón & Josh Milan – “Sunshine (I Can Fly) (Kiko Navarro Extended Remix)”* on Vega Records. A vocal that feels like morning light, a bassline that walks, and a length that lets the floor settle in. It is the kind of record Oysterdale exists to find.

There is no specific Nordic or Scandinavian chart placement or release to report this week, but the overall mood — soulful, vocal, groove-first — is the same lane Oysterdale and OKPLUS have been swimming in. That alignment matters more than a flag on the map.
